# Four-estimator causal-adjustment benchmark

Simulated cohort: n=3000, seed=2026 (see `simulate_confounded_cohort.py` for the full DGP).

True hazard ratio (treatment): **0.65**  
True risk ratio at 365 days (Monte Carlo, n=300,000): **0.7820**

The first three estimators target the hazard ratio and are directly comparable to each other and to the true HR. DoWhy has no native Cox estimator (see adr-0003) and instead targets the risk ratio at the fixed horizon above — comparable to the Monte Carlo true RR, but not on the same scale as the hazard-ratio rows.

**log bias** is `log(estimate) - log(true_value)`: closer to zero is better, and it is comparable in magnitude across the two effect-measure scales because both are ratio measures on the log scale.

**Refutation** reflects DoWhy's own `is_statistically_significant` flag (p < 0.05) applied uniformly across its three refuters, per DoWhy's own convention. For the placebo-treatment refuter specifically, a 'FAILED' result here means the original estimate differs significantly from the placebo-simulated null distribution — which some practitioners would read as *reassuring* (the effect doesn't look like noise), even though DoWhy reports it identically to a genuine robustness failure. See `docs/architecture/adr-0003-causal-model-choices.md` for the full discussion; do not read a placebo 'FAILED' here as an unambiguous problem with the estimate.
